Preregister one small native-letter objective: can the visible white counter of
an O, surrounded by ordinary MUM/MOM text, produce balanced finder structure in
the stock reader's already enabled inversion pass? Prior passive probes measured
normal black-letter binarization. They do not characterize every inversion-pass
locator run; ordinary full-payload failures still stand unchanged. This is a new
counter-topology hypothesis, not a retrospective success claim.

Exactly four native full-context finder-only layouts: installed regular ArialMT
and AmericanTypewriter, each at native20 and24px, CSSweight400, zero tracking,
normal single spaces, kerning/ligatures disabled for explicit native advances.
One continuous text node:24 rows of16 MUM words, replacing row6/word6, row6/word7
and row9/word6 with MOM (zero-based); period at each fourth row end. All letters
visible black on white. No graphic finder, hidden module, layer, invisible text,
source pixel edit, raster resizing or replacement glyph. Only three O counters
in the paragraph; no payload encoded. Repetitive words are structural material,
not natural semantic prose. Both fonts are regular body faces; keep intrinsically
heavy, styled/bold and block-ASCII results separate.

intended counter centers a square triangle. Source nominal dimension25, unit =
