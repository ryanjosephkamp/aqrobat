The three priority routes

A menu, not a sweep

40 ideas and checks

Start with the recommended routes. Conditional payload ideas remain behind the existing structure gate. Lower-priority ideas are fallbacks, not reasons to expand the run.

40 ideas visible

A. Challenge the assumptions before rendering more letters

01Count the spatial freedom in a paragraph — start here

For each proposed layout, tabulate finder pitch, body x-height, line leading, character advances, and the number of independently adjustable text regions across the data field. Draw a diagnostic overlay only after the native capture. The current tiny pitch relative to large letters may leave very few independent choices per QR module. First test: calculate this from saved PILL and one proposed article layout. Reject a claimed capacity argument that merely counts pixels or assumes every part of a glyph can be independently changed. This is a design diagnostic, not a mathematical impossibility proof.

02Audit counter-only versus complete-run pitch — start here

Compare the source counter-width model with full five-run H/V spans and ordinary dimension returns. The saved PILL arithmetic points near dimension 113 without changing the image. First test: a read-only comparison across the existing P, R, and B resources. If it suggests a new construction rule, preregister that rule before a fresh render. Do not rename the old expected dimension or use returned corners to guide a decoder. Diagonal failure remains independent.

03Identify the first failing reader stage — start here

Distinguish candidate formation, candidate ranking, triplet geometry, dimension, extraction, format information, and payload correction. Inspect existing stock returns and parity-checked passive traces first. A locator-only source naturally has no data to decode; do not call that a failed payload experiment. First test: a short stage ledger for PILL and a conventional control. A better late-stage score is not useful if an earlier stage already fails.

04Check whether our diagnostic gates are well calibrated — start here

Replay the gate calculations read-only on saved conventional controls and previously decoded outputs, including ordinary returned coordinates. Determine whether a metric is sensitive to diagonal distance convention, rounding, or a source-defined span. First test: two existing controls, no new payload render. If a checker bug exists, preserve its executed version and fix it in a fresh audited version. If a threshold appears overconservative, report that limitation; do not quietly relax it to pass a native example.

05Audit competing counters in real words

DARE showed why the intended letter is not necessarily the selected letter. Record all naturally returned candidates and their correspondence to source letters after the reader returns. First test: inspect existing PAVE/DARE/PILL traces. Later compare a fixed finder surrounded by a short readable sentence with many counters versus one with fewer. Never substitute a better candidate for the reader's actual first selection.

06Use a tradeoff ledger instead of one best score

Track actual diagonal error, H/V balance, stable span, ordinary dimension, readability, page size, and available body-text freedom together. First test: plot the existing small set; retain every case. A candidate improving one metric while losing visible letters should not displace a more useful design. This helps choose experiments; it neither changes the gates nor supplies new decoding evidence.

B. Try genuinely different native finder structures

07Choose counter topology, not a font-name sweep — high priority

Inspect at most six installed glyph resources selected for explicit structural differences: flatter counter corners, more equal H/V spans, or less surrounding ink crossing the failing diagonal. Start with visible native Latin letters. First test: small resources, then at most two justified full-context proposals. Record actual font identities. Arial round O, P/R/B appendages, Phosphate O, and logographic weight variants already have negative evidence; renaming those searches is not a new mechanism.

08Test an actual optical-size alternate — fallback

If an installed font genuinely exposes an optical-size axis or native alternate with different counter proportions, compare two declared designs at one fixed body size. This could change the topology without simply enlarging the raster. Inspect the font first; do not infer axis support or simulate it by stretching. Reject missing alternates or changes that make the letter indistinct. Keep this separate from the already-exhausted size/weight sweeps.

09Separate bowl shape from the outline treatment — fallback

A native outline can change a bowl and its exterior differently. Compare one specific native contour treatment against a matched source whose font, text, advance, and origin stay fixed. First test: two cases only, with a hypothesis about the offending diagonal segment. A generic stroke-width sweep repeats session D and is not justified. Reject overlapping letters or outlines that close the letter's distinguishing aperture.

10Let a pair of readable letters share the finder job — exploratory

Instead of asking a single counter to provide every run, test whether two ordinary adjacent letters at nonnegative spacing naturally supply complementary strokes. First test: two visible letter pairs selected from measured contours, then one real word containing a promising pair. No overprinting, clipping, per-pixel erasure, or custom QR-shaped glyph. Reject a construction that only looks correct after the letters merge into a symbol.

11Examine one specific native ligature — exploratory

A genuine OpenType ligature might join strokes while preserving a recognizable word. Inventory only installed, documented features; compare one word with its native feature enabled and disabled. The earlier Snell Roundhand work already failed, so “try cursive” alone is not new. This needs a specific measured join that addresses the actual failed axis. Reject if it requires a made-up glyph, illegible swash, or custom decoder.

12Build a finder from several words — alternative architecture

Make the target finder larger than one character, using the collective ink of readable words and normal spaces. This could bring its module scale closer to body-text control. First test: one small paragraph-sized nonpayload finder layout with a preregistered vocabulary and source layout, inspected through ordinary native binarization. Earlier bold-density prose failed phone tests; the new mechanism must address broken runs and line gaps, not repeat the old bitmap-to-bold map. Reject merged baselines or unreadably tiny words.

13Use existing page structure for a separator — exploratory

Try a paragraph indent, column gutter, or ordinary margin to provide the clear space around a native finder. First test: two full-context layouts with equal text and one changed source layout rule. These spaces must look like plausible typesetting and survive the entire page, not a crop. Reject large blank QR-shaped holes that reproduce the owner's original complaint.

14Rotate the source matrix while keeping prose upright — conditional

Once a viable native finder family exists, rotate the QR source layout to place its three finder locations at more natural paragraph openings. Keep the prose upright and regenerate source positions; do not rotate a screenshot to repair it. First test: at most two source orientations selected for the article layout. This changes where the construction is needed, not the local diagonal problem. It is not an excuse to bypass structure gates.

C. Make the geometry belong to an actual paragraph

15Large initials with readable smaller body text — high priority

Try native drop caps or larger paragraph-opening letters for the finder role, with ordinary body text carrying the data role. First test: one or two finder-only article mockups, body around 14–20 CSS pixels and an explicitly declared page width, rather than a giant repeated-word sheet. Keep the whole page native/selectable. Reject a body too small to read, and measure the scale compatibility: smaller body text is a hypothesis, not an automatic solution.

16Use three paragraph openings in a natural article layout — high priority

A two-column article can place larger initials at two upper paragraph openings and one lower opening. This supplies a plausible spatial arrangement for three finders. First test: a short complete article with real punctuation and no payload, using fixed native source positions within a readable reading order. Reject if the result requires arbitrary disconnected text islands, excessive gaps, or a normal-page view in which no words can be read.

17Give finder letters and body letters different roles — high priority

Use one already-installed native display face for initials and a readable body face for density variation. Record both actual font identities and the styling boundary. First test: compare the same article under one-font and two-font source rules. This may avoid asking a single font to solve contradictory tasks. It belongs to styled prose, not the uniform-font/plain-ASCII claim.

18Make normal-page readability a starting constraint — high priority

Declare the intended CSS page width, body size, line height, device scale, and viewing size before rendering. Aim first at a roughly 640–960 CSS-pixel article with readable body text; keep physical PNG pixels distinct from CSS pixels. First test: inspect both the whole page and a native 1:1 excerpt. Reject a success that only exists on a 10,240-square sheet or after unreadable shrinking. These suggested sizes are a working design target, not a universal readability standard or a phone result.

19Prefer a small fixed prose template over repeated control words

Use a brief coherent paragraph about printing, reading, or the experiment. Allow a finite set of sentence variants, retaining the exact visible text in the recipe. First test: two templates with similar lengths and different glyph densities. Fabricated words can remain labeled structural controls, but should not become the final phone candidate. This adds meaning without needing a language model every time the eventual tool generates an output.

20Separate fixed structure from editable prose

Identify which source positions correspond to finder/separator, timing, format, alignment, and data requirements. Give each a typography objective while all visible content remains native letters. First test: a source feasibility map before a native payload render. The map is for construction and diagnosis only; it must not become hidden modules behind text or an extraction hint. Reject a design that has no readable way to realize its mandatory regions.

D. Give the body text meaningful degrees of freedom

21Measure glyphs as small two-dimensional patterns

Do not reduce M, W, i, and punctuation to a single dark/light average. Record their native spatial occupancy, holes, joins, and baseline gaps at the chosen body size. First test: a tiny preregistered alphabet resource sheet and one word. Use these measurements to propose source text, then validate the complete native output. Source-aligned accuracy alone must not count as recovery.

22Use line-breaking dynamic programming

Choose among normal spaces and legal word breaks to move useful strokes while keeping punctuation and reading order intact. First test: a fixed 40–80-word paragraph with a small bounded set of line widths; save all source proposals. Reject clipped words, artificial internal spaces, negative tracking, or grossly ragged layouts. A solver should minimize the typography cost as well as the source target mismatch; its objective is not an acceptance reader.

23Search whole words, not arbitrary pixel replacements

Make a small vocabulary with measured native word shapes, then use a bounded beam search to fit a few local constraints in a sentence template. First test: at most a few dozen vocabulary entries and a fixed small search budget, not an unbounded dictionary search. Every candidate rendered as a full native source counts toward the run cap. Reject word substitutions that destroy readability or rely on a favorable source sampler instead of ordinary returns.

24Use controlled semantic alternatives

Let a sentence choose between a small set of grammatically valid alternatives with different ink distributions. For example, a supplied template can vary a short phrase without changing the topic. First test: one sentence and two or three alternatives, not automatic essay generation. Keep the scanner's payload fixed regardless of the visible prose. This is a later layer after structural feasibility, not a substitute for solving it.

25Try genuine font grade, if installed — styled fallback

A real grade axis is worth inspecting because it may vary ink without the same advance changes as ordinary weight. Confirm the actual installed axis before proposing a test. First test: two declared grades in an otherwise identical native sentence, with measured advances and full-context results. Do not call CSS font-weight “grade,” install a new font, or assume width preservation. Earlier bold successes and phone failures remain separate evidence.

26Use native width variants sparingly — styled fallback

An installed width axis or genuine condensed face may move strokes into a more useful arrangement while preserving words. First test: one word under two predeclared native variants, followed by a full-context proposal only if the mechanism looks plausible. No screenshot stretching or per-letter distortion. Reject strained, overlapping, or unrecognizable type; size changes alone are not a new hypothesis.

27Use ordinary punctuation and spacing as limited controls

Commas, periods, colons, paragraph breaks, and legitimate sentence alternatives can alter local density without turning the page into random punctuation. First test: one syntactically valid paragraph with two source variants. Do not insert arbitrary punctuation just to paint black modules or add spaces inside words. This offers modest freedom; it is unlikely to repair a badly broken finder by itself.

28Test visible typographic contrast as its own lane — lower priority

Different readable ink colors or emphasis styles may affect ordinary grayscale processing while preserving recognizable text. First test only after black/white mechanisms have a clear diagnosis: two visible, high-contrast source styles, without changing readers. Reject faint letters, transparent text, or a colored background pattern that secretly carries the QR. This is styled color text, not success for uniform-black prose, and camera color behavior remains untested.

E. Source-encoding choices, only after the finder gate

29Choose a mask compatible with the text — conditional

The standard encoder exposes eight mask choices. Score their source patterns against the measured text constraints, retain the matrix-level scores for all eight, and render at most the few preregistered finalists allowed by the total payload cap. Use ordinary readers on complete native outputs; no mask hints. Do not claim all eight were experimentally tested if only their matrices were compared. See the Nayuki API.

30Co-design version and error correction with page scale — conditional

Compare a small fixed pair of standard version/ECC choices for the exact same payload. A higher ECC setting can demand more spatial detail; a larger symbol can demand a less readable page. First test: source feasibility calculations, then at most two native proposals if the gates and cap permit. Do not assume maximum ECC is automatically best or that it repairs locator failure. Keep actual requested/encoded settings in the recipe.

31Vary legal segment choices without changing decoded text — conditional

Different standards-valid segment choices can produce different matrices while representing the same string. First test: a small pair supported by the pinned encoder and ordinary conventional controls. Compare exact decoded bytes/text, not merely equivalent URLs. Do not use arbitrary padding or undocumented decoder tolerance to gain design freedom. This is optional source flexibility, not permission to rewrite the encoder or the payload.

32Solve timing and format regions explicitly — conditional

A source with recognizable finder centers may still fail before reading data. Construct readable native strokes for the mandatory timing/format regions and diagnose their ordinary returned behavior separately. First test: one gated full-payload proposal with a post-return region error ledger. No forced extraction or repairing the raster. A pretty data field does not compensate for a missing way to locate or interpret it.

33Diagnose damage at the codeword level — conditional

After an ordinary reader attempts the unchanged source, use known source geometry only for a clearly labeled post-return error analysis. Determine whether a typography change concentrates or spreads errors across codewords. First test: one failed gated payload and its conventional control. Do not feed a repaired matrix back into an acceptance reader, count diagnostic decoding as success, or assume a percentage of bad modules equals an ECC budget.

34Learn from QArt, but keep its payload tradeoff explicit — later

QArt demonstrates that encoding choices can be designed around imagery. Its illustrated numeric URL fragment changes the decoded string, so that method is outside this run's exact-payload rule. First step here is theoretical: inventory only standards-valid freedom that leaves the payload unchanged. Do not promise arbitrary artistic freedom or start a Reed–Solomon optimizer during this bounded run. This could become a separate research proposal if the native-text route merits it.

F. Make a success useful and credible

35Freeze a candidate before an additional ordinary reader

After the normal gates pass, retain a frozen source/PNG/recipe and test an already-installed implementation not used to choose that candidate, if one is available within budget. OpenCV, ZXing, jsQR, ZBar, and Apple Vision are implementation families; multiple profiles of one are not independent engines. Record unknowns/timeouts separately. At least one exact acceptance result must come from outside ZXing; do not install a new stack simply to collect badges.

36Recreate from source, not from a repaired screenshot

Save the actual text, styling, font identity, layout recipe, payload, and native capture settings. First test after a promising result: a fresh native render and the same ordinary readers. Retain its hash even if it differs, and explain the difference. If a result depends on one accidental raster alignment, report it as fragile rather than reproducible. Do not replace the old outcome.

37Test one realistic native display variation

If a candidate survives recreation, preregister one modest native CSS-size or device-scale variation as a separate capture, subject to the same readability and file limits. Feed that complete native output to ordinary readers. This tests a display condition, not phone optics. Do not blur, crop, or resample the failed original and call the altered image the unchanged candidate.

38Test the saved text document's layout contract

For a successful styled candidate, verify selection/copy text identity and reopening its self-contained native HTML. Record whether fixed line breaks, fonts, and styles are essential. First test: one reopen in an owned browser profile, without touching the product or extension. This does not establish plain-text paste fidelity in Gmail, TextEdit, or another device. Preserve that distinction in the report.

39Review the paragraph without a decoder overlay

Inspect the unannotated whole page at its stated reading size before looking at diagnostic grids. Can the words be read in order? Does it look like an article rather than three decorative initials beside noise? First test: a local visual review with concrete rejection notes and a few quoted words that are actually visible. Do not simulate owner approval or replace it with a mechanical overlap metric. A scanner pass and a prose pass are separate tests.

40Prepare one honest phone-review package

Only after exact independent recovery and readability review, provide one or two best candidates with the expected full URL, declared viewing size, original native output, and brief steps for Samsung Camera and the dedicated scanner. No preprocessed scan helper should be presented as the actual artifact. Keep failed cases available in evidence, but do not ask Ryan to repeat a large failed grid. No phone result exists until he reports one.

Evidence, limits, and references

What is known, and what is only a hypothesis

Session D retained 15 native captures and 15 exact immediate-repeat receipts. All strict structure gates failed; no new source encoded a payload. PILL had stable central rows and improved balanced spans, but its worst true-diagonal error was 0.5563, above the unchanged 0.35 gate. Its ordinary dimension was 113 versus the source model's 125. Nothing new is a phone candidate.

There is a concrete scale hypothesis to audit. PILL's saved glyph resource has horizontal runs [6,4,12,4,6] and vertical runs [4,4,9,4,4]. Averaging their total spans divided by seven gives 4.0714 pixels. With the saved horizontal finder-center separation of 430.59375 pixels, distance / pitch + 7 is about 112.76, near the ordinary dimension 113. This may explain part of the dimension mismatch. It does not fix the diagonal, prove correct finder selection, or justify retroactively relabeling a failed source. Any new source model must be declared before rendering a fresh case. Earlier phases already used related run-based models; this is a targeted audit, not a claim of invention.

The body-text question is equally important: the current 64-pixel letters and roughly 108-pixel leading are much larger than a roughly four-pixel module. That mismatch is a reason to measure spatial freedom before building another payload. It is not a general impossibility proof.

Run limits
  • At most 180 minutes including intake, verification, commit/push, and handback. Start no new experiment after minute 160; finish cleanup by minute 180.
  • At most 24 new full native source proposals across the run, including rejected proposals and any payload cases; at most four payload proposals, conditional on the existing gates. At most one immediate repeat per captured case.
  • At most 40 MB of new logical files in total, including source, reports, captures, diagnostics, and failures; normally at most 6 MB per fresh phase, never more than 8 MB when preregistered with a reason. Reserve 5 MB overall and at least 20 minutes for closing work. No evicting outcomes to make room.
  • At most 24 small native glyph-resource renders, each at most 512 × 512. Full native captures at most four megapixels / 16 MB uncompressed RGBA. All resource renders, controls, profiles, and repeats have separate counts.
  • One owned browser and one CPU-heavy job at a time; reuse installed tools and fonts. No downloads of fonts/models, new dependencies, paid services, or long-running background work. Use small bounded search spaces and timeouts.
  • These are ceilings, not a target denominator. Preregister each small batch before execution, including its conditional and unattempted slots. If a budget cannot fit a useful next case, seal the work rather than bypass it.
