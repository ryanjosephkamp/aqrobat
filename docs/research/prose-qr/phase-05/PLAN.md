## Hypothesis and frozen first batch

Average ink-mass optimization does not ensure the corner patterns survive
ordinary binarization. Measure actual installed glyph shapes at a native
20-pixel font size, then compare an ink-mass objective with a spatial objective
that emphasizes the intended finder regions. Source geometry may guide rendering
and later diagnostics, but never repair a raster or force an acceptance decoder.

The first batch has **16 native text candidates**:

- Installed Menlo and Courier New, each at uniform weight 400 or uniform 700.
- Uniform letter spacing 0 or -2 pixels; no horizontal/vertical raster resizing.
- Ink-mass objective or glyph-pixel/finder objective.
- Three character advances across and two text lines per square QR region.
- Exact `https://example.com/`, Q ECC, boost off, automatic mask, five-region border.
- Fabricated whole-word forms with vowels, single spaces, punctuation, and fixed
  complete lines. Semantic coherence is not claimed. No per-letter color/weight,
  font variation, QR underlay, invisible text, blur, outlines, or decoder tuning.
- Exclude glyphs taller than the chosen line height, to avoid vertical ink overlap.
  Negative tracking may crowd letters; inspect legibility and reject unreadable
  output even if a reader passes. Uniform 700 is a separate typography condition,
  not the regular-weight goal. Tracking is a recorded compatibility requirement.

Record metrics and actual platform faces before generating candidates. Measure
the native DOM baseline for each line-height condition; the spatial objective
uses installed glyph pixels at their measured positions. Approximate glyph
composition is an optimization model, not evidence about the final raster.
